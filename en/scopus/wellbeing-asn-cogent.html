Enhancing well-being in hybrid work: the crucial role of organizational support for Indonesia's State Civil Apparatus

Despinur Dara (first author), Susan Febriantina (Universitas Negeri Jakarta), and Suwatno Suwatno (Universitas Pendidikan Indonesia).

Vol. 12 No. 1 (2025), article 2454084 · Open access · 6500+ views, 7 citations · DOI: 10.1080/23311908.2025.2454084 · Journal page

In brief

The paper that opened this line of hybrid work research tests how hybrid working, perceived organizational support, and job burnout affect the well-being of Indonesian civil servants. The story it tells is coherent: hybrid work raises burnout, while organizational support reduces burnout and strengthens well-being at the same time. The benefit of hybrid work for well-being only appears once organizational support enters the picture.

In one line: hybrid work is neither automatically good nor bad for staff. Organizational support is what decides which way it goes.

Based on a survey of 533 civil servants analysed with structural equation modelling. Five of six hypotheses were supported, and the one that failed turned out to be the most interesting of all.
