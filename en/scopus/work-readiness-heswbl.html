Beyond digital skills: work-integrated learning, adaptability, and the thresholds for graduate work readiness

Despinur Dara (first author, Universitas Negeri Jakarta), Tuong Thien Nguyen (Foreign Trade University, Hanoi, Vietnam), and Donny Maha Putra (Universitas Pembangunan Nasional Veteran Jakarta).

Quantitative research article · DOI: 10.1108/HESWBL-10-2025-0446 · Journal page


      

        
      

    

  

  

  

    

      
In brief

      
This study examines how work-integrated learning shapes graduate work readiness, separating two capabilities that are often lumped together: digital literacy as an upstream enabler, and technological adaptability as the proximal driver. It also calculates the minimum capability thresholds students must cross to be genuinely ready for work.
The provocative finding: knowing how to operate digital tools does not by itself make graduates work ready. What matters is the ability to adapt when the technology changes.
Based on a survey of 520 final-year Indonesian undergraduates with at least three months of work-integrated learning, analysed with structural equation modelling and Necessary Condition Analysis.
