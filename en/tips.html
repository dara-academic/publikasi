What reviewers flag most often

These five turned up again and again across the review documents for all six works. If your time is short, fix these first.

1. A discussion thin on references

This is complaint number one. One reviewer called a discussion resting on only one or two references a fundamental weakness, and used the word worrying. A discussion is not a repeat of the results, it is where your findings are set against other people's.

2. A research gap that rests only on a change of setting

Saying that earlier studies looked at the private sector while yours looks at the public sector is often judged insufficient. Explain why the change of setting alters how the phenomenon works, rather than simply noting that the location differs.

3. A method that cannot be retraced

Reviewers ask in fine detail: how the sample size was decided, how respondents were contacted, how bias was controlled, how long data collection ran and why. For qualitative work they also want the interview guide along with the rationale behind each question.

4. A literature review that summarises rather than weighs

Listing earlier studies in sequence does not count as a critical review. Reviewers also specifically ask for contradictory findings to be included, not only the ones that support your hypotheses.

5. A missing ethics statement

One editor stated plainly that the journal does not publish work which cannot evidence adherence to institutional ethics policies. Prepare that statement while you are still writing the method, rather than waiting to be asked.
